Methodology

Zentra assesses each vendor domain using passive checks against publicly available sources. No intrusive testing, authentication attempt or exploitation of any kind is performed against a vendor's systems.

Checks performed

  • TLS / certificate — certificate validity and expiry, protocol versions and cipher strength.
  • Breach history — publicly catalogued data breaches associated with the vendor's domain (breach metadata only; no account-level data is retrieved or stored).
  • Internet exposure — services already indexed as publicly reachable by third-party internet-scan data.
  • Known vulnerabilities — published CVEs matched to software versions the vendor's own servers disclose.
  • Email / DNS security — SPF, DMARC, DKIM discovery and CAA records.
  • Web hardening — browser security response headers.

Scoring

Each check produces a normalized result with a status, a severity and a confidence value. Results roll up into six weighted categories that total 100 points: TLS/certificate (25), breach history (20), internet exposure (20), known vulnerabilities (15), email/DNS security (15) and web hardening (5). Additional findings within one category contribute progressively less, so a single noisy category cannot dominate the score. A conclusive critical-severity finding sets a minimum score of 50; two or more set a minimum of 75.

Handling of missing data

A check that could not be completed — because a data source was unavailable, or because the information is not discoverable from public sources — is recorded as “not assessed”. It contributes no risk points and is never treated as a security failure. Instead it reduces the assessment's coverage and confidence, both of which are reported. Where coverage is too thin to be meaningful, Zentra declines to publish a risk level rather than presenting an unreliable one.

Confidence and provenance

Every finding records the source it came from, when it was observed and how confident Zentra is in it. Findings derived from a vendor's self-declared software version are indicators rather than proof: a vendor may have applied a backported fix that the declared version does not reflect.

Important. Zentra's risk scores are informational assessments based on signals available from public sources. They should not be interpreted as legal, regulatory, audit or certification advice, and they are not a substitute for a security review, a penetration test or a formal vendor due-diligence process.

This report is intended to support a vendor risk management process and to provide evidence of ongoing third-party monitoring. It does not itself confer, demonstrate or guarantee compliance with any standard or certification scheme. Where a specific control requirement applies to your organization, confirm the position with your auditor or adviser.

Findings reflect what was observable at the time of assessment. The absence of a finding is not a guarantee that no issue exists.
